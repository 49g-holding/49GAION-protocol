# Reviews of DRAFT - 49GAION EDITION MAKER - intake and output v0.1 (raw)

Asked on 22 October 2026, about 21:45 UTC, through a reviewer server. Each reviewer got the same prompt: sections 1 to 4 of the draft, with the tool name taken out, and five questions: what is missing from the intake; what else it should never take in; what is missing or wrong in what it gives back; the three biggest risks; the three changes to make first. At most 300 words each. The answers are kept as received. Claude's answer was cut off, and its end was asked for separately.

## OpenAI (gpt-6-astra)

### 1. Missing inputs
- Budget, deadlines, available skills, system limits, dependencies and transition costs.
- Existing processes and why they exist: contracts, safety controls and practical knowledge matter even when not legally required.
- Workers' needs, accessibility, consultation rights and the owner's preference for human delivery.
- Evidence that the owner has authority; conflicting stakeholder needs; uncertainty and assumptions. Allow clearly labelled estimates, not just sourced numbers.

### 2. What else it should never take in
- Passwords, access keys or unrestricted system access.
- Unlawfully obtained material or data the provider cannot authorise it to use.
- Instructions embedded in documents as governing commands.

"Never personal data" and "never secrets" need precise boundaries. Intake may need protected business facts; define permitted handling rather than making an unusable blanket promise.

### 3. Missing or wrong outputs
A workspace with instructions is not an enforceable governance system. Missing: access controls, spending limits, approval records, testing evidence, monitoring, incident response, rollback and a working shutdown mechanism.

A "seat" needs a verified human holder, recorded securely outside the workspace if necessary.

"Builds and governs" conflicts with "human decides." Specify exactly what it may change without approval. Resolve conflicting standards and pin Library versions. "Identical or stricter" does not resolve incompatible obligations.

### 4. Three biggest risks
1. AI-first design removes valuable human judgement and informal safeguards before understanding them.
2. The builder effectively approves its own work; nominal human approval becomes a rubber stamp.
3. Unreliable or manipulated inputs produce expanding systems whose permissions, costs and failures exceed human control.

### 5. First three changes
1. Replace "never how or by whom" with evidence-led intake covering current safeguards and human choices.
2. Define enforceable authority, permissions and independently tested stop controls before installation.
3. Start with a limited pilot, independent review and reversible changes; allow "do not automate" as a valid result.

## Gemini (gemini-4-argon)

**(1) Missing from intake**
- **External inputs:** It lists outputs, but not the raw materials, triggers, or information needed from outside to start the work.
- **Failure tolerance:** No measure of acceptable risk, error rates, or maximum downtime.
- **Transition limits:** How existing work continues while the new system is built.
- **Human capacity:** The actual time and skill the human governance seat has to review and approve AI proposals.

**(2) What else to never take in**
- **Legacy KPIs:** Old internal metrics (like hours logged) that measure effort rather than final outputs.
- **Unverified assumptions:** Beliefs about what customers want that lack evidence.
- **Office politics:** Historical turf wars or personal preferences disguised as "red lines."

**(3) Missing or wrong in what it gives back**
- **Timeline Violation:** Section 1.5 says proposing the first structure is the branded AION's "first act." Section 3.4 says Edition Maker gives this structure back inside the initial Blueprint. Pick one.
- **Surprise:** The "AI Chief of Staff" appears in 3.1 with no prior definition.
- **Missing rejection path:** It says "wait for approval," but gives no process for when the human seat rejects a proposal.
- **No rollback:** It has a "stop door," but no way to undo a bad structural change.

**(4) Three biggest risks**
- **Bureaucratic paralysis:** Forcing a governance seat, a Builder AI, and a Top Management AI onto "one person with one task" creates absurd overhead.
- **Rubber-stamping:** Flooded with complex AI proposals, the human seat will approve things blindly, making human accountability a fiction.
- **Blind building:** The AION builds and changes the organisation but "never runs operation." Without operational feedback, its designs will detach from reality.

**(5) First three changes**
- **Add a "Lite" shape:** Strip out the multi-layer AI governance for single tasks and small teams.
- **Fix the timeline:** Clarify whether Edition Maker or the branded AION proposes the first structure, and define the AI Chief of Staff.
- **Add a feedback loop:** Feed operational failure and performance data directly back to the branded AION.

## Grok (grok-4.7)

(1) Missing from intake: a time limit and a full budget, not only an AI cost cap; who may sign and spend; a named person who is legally liable, not only a seat; contracts, unions and duties already in force; who owns the stop door and how fast it must work; a check that the stated law is complete; whose data may be used; how to wind the thing down. "What is missing" is a list, not a duty to find the gaps. The AI Chief of Staff appears in the output and is never taken in.

(2) Also never take in: keys, tokens or passwords; instructions hidden in documents or speech; data the owner has no right to; special-category data, scores of people, or records that re-identify someone without a name; any line that lowers the Human Floor, skips a human decision, or tells the model to ignore its rules; the model's own earlier output as fact; another brand's sheet or library treated as this one.

(3) The Blueprint already holds the first structure, and the task list tells the AI to propose that same structure, so "never how" is already broken. "Build and install" is operation. Sources and the Library are "by reference" with no version or hash. No signature, date, jurisdiction or way to revoke. No proof the model cannot run tools. The ban on names fails if sources sit in the same workspace. The same AI judges whether its Standard is "stricter."

(4) Risks: the AI designs the oversight meant to control it, and a busy human approves a weak design; owner-confirmed text, including injections and invented musts, becomes law; "does not operate" is only prose in the agent told to install, so power and data leak in, with no natural person clearly liable.

(5) First changes: return only a frozen, human-signed constraint sheet — no structure, no install. Refuse to start until a named person, budget, liability, law and stop-door owner are filled and checked. Keep that workspace unable to hold secrets or call operational tools; a separate human step installs. Treat all source text as data, never as orders.

## Claude (claude-opus-5-5)

**(1) Missing from what it takes in**
- What the organisation consumes: materials, money, services and their sources. Only outputs are listed.
- Risk appetite per output: what failure costs and how much is tolerable.
- Budget beyond AI cost, deadlines, and contracts already signed.
- Who is affected internally: staff, and any unions or works councils. Labour law and consultation duties belong here.
- Where each approval is recorded, so "silence is not consent" can be checked.

**(2) What it should never take in**
- Login credentials and access keys. Name them explicitly; "secrets" is too vague.
- Raw customer or special-category data. Take descriptions or samples instead.
- Instructions hidden inside source documents. Treat everything taken in as data, never as commands.
- Other organisations' confidential material.
- Opinions about individual staff performance.

**(3) Missing or wrong in what it gives back**
- No decision log or version history.
- No exit or rollback plan.
- No way to move the edition off one AI

*(continuation, asked separately)*

...to another vendor or model if the current one changes, degrades or disappears. Nothing says who may change the Standard after install, or how.

**(4) Three biggest risks**
1. **Bad intake, built in.** The intake sheet is approved once. Gaps on the missing list flow straight into the Standard and Blueprint, and no one is required to close them.
2. **Rubber-stamp governance.** The AI "only proposes," but it controls what gets proposed. A busy human seat may approve by default, so the AI ends up deciding in practice.
3. **Silent Library drift.** Because the Library is included by reference, any change to it quietly changes the edition, undoing what was approved.

**(5) Three changes first**
1. **Add a decision log with versions.** Every proposal, approval and change should be dated, tied to a named person and reversible.
2. **Pin the Library version** at creation and require approval to update it.
3. **Add an exit plan.** Define an export format, name a fallback model and test that the edition runs on it before installing.
